# Copy Guidelines v0

Applies to all user-facing text, in Arabic and English, including Noor. Review and Urgent templates need clinician review before final use.

## Tone
Warm, calm, short sentences, plain words. Talk about "what we measure," never about the person's brain or mind being good or bad. Always offer a next step.

## Banned words in user-facing text
diagnosis, diagnose, dementia, Alzheimer's (in Watch or Stable), impaired, impairment, failed, abnormal, decline (use "change"), score (use "result" or the weekly story), test (prefer "activity" or "check").

Clinician-facing screens may use clinical terms.

## Always
- Show uncertainty in words ("this may be due to…", "we are not sure yet").
- Say it is screening, not a check-up or diagnosis, on Stable and Review cards.
- Name the context factor when one explains a change.
- Let the person decide whether to share.

## Templates (draft)

**Stable:** "No meaningful change in what we measure. This is not a full health check. If anything worries you, talk to your doctor any time."

**Watch, context-explained:** "Your activities were a little different this week. Your sleep was shorter than usual, which often affects how we feel and focus. Try [tip]. We'll do a short check again on [date]."

**Watch, unexplained:** "Some of your recent results were different from your usual. This happens for many reasons. We'll do a few short checks over the next days to understand it better, and we may ask [informant] a few questions if you agree."

**Review:** "Over the last few checks, some results have stayed different from your usual. This is not a diagnosis. It is a good reason to talk with a doctor, who can look at the full picture. Would you like to share a summary with your clinic?"

**Urgent:** "What you described needs urgent medical help. Please call 998 now or go to the nearest emergency department. If someone is with you, ask them to help." (No other content. Never generated by the language model.)

**Genomics (user):** "Your genetic information has been shared with your clinician, who can explain what it means. Genes are only one part of the picture, and healthy habits help everyone."
